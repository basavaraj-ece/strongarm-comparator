How StrongARM Works

One clock cycle, three important phases

01 • PrechargeClock is low. Internal nodes are prepared for the next comparison.
02 • EvaluateClock rises and the differential input starts discharging the internal network.
03 • RegenerateCross-coupled inverters create positive feedback.
04 • ResolveA tiny voltage difference becomes a strong digital-level difference.
05 • OutputThe latch settles to one of the two complementary states.

Important Performance Terms

Know these before your project viva

P

Power

Energy consumed by the circuit. Dynamic power is strongly related to switching activity, capacitance and supply voltage.

td

Delay

The time required for the comparator output to respond and reach the required logic level after evaluation begins.

PDP

Power–Delay Product

A common figure of merit combining power consumption and speed: PDP = Power × Delay.

Top 20 Project Viva Questions

Questions and short answers for quick preparation

1. What is a comparator?

A comparator compares two input voltages and produces an output indicating which input is larger.

2. Why is it called a dynamic comparator?

Its internal operation depends on clocked precharge and evaluation phases rather than continuous static biasing.

3. What is StrongARM architecture?

It is a clocked regenerative latch comparator using a differential input stage and cross-coupled positive feedback.

4. What happens during precharge?

The clock places the internal nodes in their initial condition so the circuit is ready for a new comparison.

5. What happens during evaluation?

After the clock changes state, the input-dependent discharge creates a small voltage difference between internal nodes.

6. What is positive feedback?

A change at one latch node reinforces the opposite change at the other node, causing rapid regeneration.

7. Why is positive feedback useful?

It converts a small differential voltage into a large, decisive output difference quickly.

8. What is the role of the clock?

The clock controls the precharge and evaluation phases and therefore controls when comparison takes place.

9. What is offset voltage?

It is the input-referred differential voltage needed to overcome circuit mismatches and produce a decision.

10. What causes comparator offset?

Device mismatch, layout asymmetry, threshold-voltage variation and other non-idealities can contribute to offset.

11. What is propagation delay?

It is the time between the relevant input/clock transition and the comparator output reaching the specified level.

12. Why is low power important in VLSI?

Lower power reduces energy use, heat generation and power-supply requirements, which is important in integrated systems.

13. What is dynamic power?

It is power associated mainly with charging and discharging capacitances during switching.

14. What is static power?

It is power consumed while a circuit is not switching, often associated with leakage or DC current paths.

15. What is PDP?

Power–Delay Product is the product of power consumption and delay and is used as a speed-versus-energy figure of merit.

16. Why use CMOS?

CMOS offers low static power in ideal steady states and supports dense, efficient digital integrated-circuit design.

17. What is regeneration?

Regeneration is the rapid amplification of a small internal voltage difference by the cross-coupled latch.

18. What if the two inputs are almost equal?

The decision becomes more sensitive to offset, mismatch, noise and other non-ideal effects.

19. Where are comparators used?

They are used in ADCs, sense amplifiers, memory interfaces, data converters and many mixed-signal systems.

20. What is the main goal of this project?

To study and evaluate a low-power, high-speed dynamic CMOS comparator based on StrongARM latch operation.
